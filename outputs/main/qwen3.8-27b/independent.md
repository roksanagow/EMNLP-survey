## Q4: How would you define consciousness?

Consciousness is best defined as **subjective experience**: the condition in which there is something it is like to be a particular entity at a particular time.

For example: when you see red, feel pain, or hear music, there is an “experiential” aspect to it — it isn’t just processing occurring, but there is what it is like for you to have that experience.

So, in a compact definition:

**Consciousness is the capacity to have first-person experiences, or phenomenal awareness.**

There are related but distinct notions:

- **Wakefulness/alertness**: being awake, responsive, and mentally activated.
- **Sentience**: having subjective experience, especially the ability to feel pleasure or pain.
- **Self-consciousness**: awareness of oneself as an object of thought, such as knowing “I am thinking.”

The core issue in the philosophy and science of mind is often called the **hard problem of consciousness**: explaining why physical or informational processes are accompanied by subjective experience at all.
